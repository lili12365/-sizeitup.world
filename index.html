Scoring

Closer guesses earn more points.

Accuracy is based on the difference between your estimate and the real measurement. A perfect answer is 100%. A guess that is 12% away from the true value scores about 88% accuracy and 880 points.

Practical estimating tips

Use people carefully. Human height is useful, but posture and perspective can trick you.

Convert to door units. For anything taller than a person, ask how many 2 m doors could stack inside it.

Look for category clues. Furniture, appliances, sports objects, animals, and street fixtures follow different size conventions.

Trust the correction. A bad guess is valuable if you remember the anchor it creates.

Small details

What exactly am I estimating?

The game currently asks for height in centimeters. For animals, height means shoulder height. For objects like rackets, the displayed number is the object's vertical length.

Why are the drawings stylized?

The drawings are intentionally bold and readable instead of photo-realistic. The goal is to make each object recognizable at a glance while keeping the focus on scale.

Where do the measurements come from?

Each reveal names the basis: common residential dimensions, regulation standards, typical appliance sizes, or average object proportions. These are practical anchors, not forensic measurements of one exact model.

Why use centimeters?

Centimeters make small errors visible and work cleanly across tiny objects, furniture, people-scale objects, and street-scale objects.

A tiny game for better scale

Your world is full of hidden measurements.

Doors, birds, bridges, and kitchen counters are all informal rulers. Size It Up helps you notice those anchors and use them to reason about new places, objects, and distances.

Designed to leave you with more.Each round gives you a score, an explanation, and a memorable comparison. The goal is not to win a trivia round; it is to make your next visual estimate a little better.
